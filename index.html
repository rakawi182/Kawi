Input guide
Long vowelsaa ii uu or ā ī ū
Other vowelsai au e o; pepet (ĕ): E or ĕ; ö: Ö or ö; ṛ: R or ṛ
Special consonantsType ṅ ñ ś ṣ ṭ ḍ ṇ directly (IAST). ng, ny, sh read as n+g, n+y, s+h unless the ASCII shortcut is enabled (then ṅ, ñ, ś). S=ṣ; T Th D Dh N=ṭ ṭh ḍ ḍh ṇ; jñ=jña; aspirates: kh gh ch jh th dh ph bh
Signsŋ/M=anusvara, H/ḥ=visarga, ~=candrabindu, |=danda, ||=double danda
SeparationAn apostrophe ' prevents letters from merging (e.g. n'g = n+g when ASCII shortcuts are on); r'k forces a plain RA instead of repha. Other capital letters are lowercased automatically
Automatic rulesConsecutive consonants receive a conjoiner (conjunct); a word-final consonant receives a killer; r before a consonant becomes repha
KeypadToggle Keypad for console mode: both screens fill the viewport and a docked aksara keypad replaces the system keyboard. Keys insert Kawi glyphs when the Kawi box is the input (Kawi → Latin), or IAST when the Latin box is the input; vowel-sign keys replace an inherent a, and the conjoiner/killer keys remove it
Cancellation{la} marks a cancelled syllable: the signs -i and -u are stacked on it (e.g. ka{la}ta)
Conjunct ṛk'ṛ = consonant + conjoiner + letter vocalic R (after a morpheme boundary); kṛ = vowel sign (word-internal). Unicode has no vowel signs for ḷ, ṝ, ḹ, so kḷ kṝ kḹ = consonant + conjoiner + letter
Script styleSettings → Script style: alternate -ā (pa, ṅa, ṭa), final -r as repha (encoded at the start of the syllable's cluster, as in the gebang texts; reading it back is a heuristic: a repha before a cluster with a vowel sign is read as final -r), double-e ai/au, composite long vowels, gemination by -ā. Round trip Kawi → Latin follows the same switches

Kakawin excerpts tap to open

Tap an excerpt to load it into the console. Transliterations follow Jákl (2020), Archipel 100, which cites the editions of Worsley et al. (2013), Teeuw and Robson (2005), and Robson (2016). The Indonesian translations are free renderings written for this page. The Wṛttasañcaya (Tanakung) and Rāmāyaṇa verses follow the readings printed in Hunter (2001), BKI 157, normalized to this page’s IAST; check them against Kern (1875) and Hooykaas before citing. The verses on poets and the experience of beauty (laṅö), and Bhāratayuddha 10.12–13, are quoted from the passages printed in Zoetmulder (1957), BKI 113, normalized to this page’s IAST from a scanned text; several are half-verses, and kṣatriya in Bhāratayuddha 10.13 is normalized from the scan’s kṣinatriya. Fragments from Worsley (2022), Wacana 23(2), are quoted only as printed there with their canto.verse references (letters a–d mark the quarter-verse, pāda); they are not complete stanzas, and the Indonesian renderings follow Worsley’s English glosses. Verify against the critical editions before citing. Use the filter to show one kakawin.

Kakawin
Manggala opening stanzas · tap to load

The manggala is the invocation that opens a kakawin. The three below are the ones quoted with a translation in Zoetmulder (1957), “Kawi and kekawin”, BKI 113: 64–68, normalized to this page’s IAST from a scanned text. Sumanasāntaka is given as the continuous passage printed there, one segment per line, each with its gloss. Kālāntaka and Śiwarātrikalpa (Lubdhaka) are quoted there as a single line each, so only that line is given here; no stanza numbers are printed in the source. The Indonesian renderings follow Zoetmulder’s English glosses; the Kālāntaka line is his paraphrase of its sense. Check against the critical editions before citing.

The Noto Sans Kawi font (SIL OFL 1.1) is embedded, so this page works offline. Conjuncts and repha are shaped by the font; U+11F5A KAWI SIGN NUKTA (Unicode 18.0) is not yet in Noto Sans Kawi 1.000, so its tile stays hidden until the embedded font includes it, so their appearance depends on the typeface. The Bold, Stone inscription, and Lontar styles are display effects on the same font, not authentic palaeographic forms.
